# Chapter 1. Methods for reviewing academic integrity signals

When two students submit similar work, the teacher needs to see what they shared and whether the assignment itself supplied it. When two students finish a quiz together, the teacher needs to know how often classmates do the same under a common deadline. These questions require different evidence. The first has a candidate source and a passage to inspect. The second requires a reference group and an account of the observations that made a particular attempt unusual. This distinction guides the choice of methods in Examory.

## Finding reused passages

A student can copy a paragraph into a much longer essay while sharing little of the essay's total vocabulary with the source. Broder's distinction between resemblance and containment explains the difficulty. Converting successive words to overlapping token sequences, or shingles, preserves local wording. The Jaccard coefficient measures the fraction of distinct shingles shared by the two sets. A MinHash signature estimates this fraction. Comparing windows gives a short passage a chance to appear among the candidates even when whole essays have little in common [@broder1997resemblance].

Locality-sensitive hashing makes retrieval cheaper by looking for collisions between signatures [@gionis1999similarity]. An omitted source never reaches the teacher, however accurate the subsequent comparison may be. Examory therefore measures both the proportion of annotated source pairs retrieved and the proportion surviving exact verification. It preserves offsets into the submitted text so a reviewer can inspect a match in context. Quotations and teacher-supplied instructions are useful tests of what a high lexical score can mean in an assignment. [Text and code review](../05_literature/plagiarism_detection.md)

Term weighting offers a cheaper comparator. TF-IDF downweights common vocabulary, and cosine compares document vectors even when their lengths differ [@salton1988term]. The score gives little indication of which sentences carried the overlap. Sentence encoders such as Sentence-BERT can retrieve passages that express a similar idea in different words [@reimers2019sentence]. For an exercise that asks every student to define the same concept, that property can surface many independently correct answers. A passage retrieval experiment must therefore include legitimate answers to the same prompt.

## Finding code fragments that survive editing

Formatting a Java method or renaming a local variable can alter many characters while preserving the sequence of operations. A lexer can map names and literals to common token classes and retain the source coordinates of each token. Winnowing selects representative fingerprints from windows of token sequences, giving a way to recover matching regions even when the surrounding methods differ [@schleimer2003winnowing]. JPlag compares language-aware token sequences using Greedy String Tiling [@prechelt2002jplag]. Both approaches give a teacher identifiable pieces of code to compare. A required method signature or a short conventional loop can also produce such a match.

Abstract syntax trees describe program structure more directly. Subtree comparison can recover clones despite changes to spelling or layout, at the cost of a parser and normalization rules for each language [@baxter1998clone]. CodeBERT learns representations for both natural language and program code. Its cited evaluation covers code search and documentation generation, leaving clone retrieval as a separate evaluation problem [@feng2020codebert]. Examory selects normalized tokens and winnowing for its first code experiment. Comparing fingerprints against exact token-sequence equality makes the effect of renaming and local edits measurable. ## Indicators of generated writing

GLTR displays the ranks that a language model assigns to the author's token choices [@gehrmann2019gltr]. DetectGPT scores how the probability of a passage changes under small perturbations [@mitchell2023detectgpt]. Both depend on the reference model. A classifier trained on the human and generated responses in HC3 can also learn the conventions of that collection's questions, which makes evaluation on a held-out prompt or generator particularly important [@guo2023hc3].

Watermarking answers a narrower question. A compatible generator changes its token choices according to a known scheme, and a detector tests the resulting pattern [@kirchenbauer2023watermark]. Stylometry uses features such as function words or punctuation to compare writing habits [@stamatatos2009survey]. A change in genre can alter those habits, just as a writer's limited English vocabulary can produce text that a model finds easy to predict. Liang and colleagues documented false positives when AI-text detectors scored human-written TOEFL essays [@liang2023bias]. For Examory, the first outcome of interest is therefore the false-positive rate on human submissions, including its variation by language background where suitable consented data exist. AI-origin indicators remain an offline research question. [AI-text review and evaluation decision](../05_literature/plagiarism_detection.md)

## Interpreting assessment behaviour

Response time depends on both the person and the item. A lognormal response-time model accounts for differences in learner speed and the time that a question requires [@vanderlinden2006lognormal]. Examory can compare a first response with a reference for a comparable question and inspect revisions separately. For an abrupt improvement in score, the meaningful comparison is the same student's earlier performance on a comparable assessment. The absence of an earlier attempt leaves that comparison unavailable.

Isolation Forest gives high anomaly scores to observations that random splits isolate quickly [@liu2008isolation]. One-Class SVM estimates a boundary around normal observations [@scholkopf2001support], while Local Outlier Factor considers the density around a student relative to nearby observations [@breunig2000lof]. A group copying from one another can be internally consistent and look locally ordinary. Autoencoders offer another score through reconstruction error, but can learn to reconstruct behaviours present in their training data, including undesirable ones [@sakurada2014autoencoders]. A direct comparison with training-quantile rules is therefore necessary before the extra complexity of a multivariate model is justified.

Wollack's response-copying statistic accounts for how likely a match is on a particular item [@wollack1997copying]. The same consideration applies to Examory's simpler screening rule. Two attempts submitted close together are more informative when their final answers also coincide on aligned questions. A matching answer hash establishes equality of the recorded choices. Calling a match an unusual shared error would require an answer key and a reference distribution. [Behavioural evidence and comparators](../05_literature/anomaly_detection.md)

## Video evidence and limits on its use

MediaPipe provides a framework for video perception [@lugaresi2019mediapipe]. Facial geometry can be estimated from a monocular stream [@kartynnik2019geometry], and the Face Landmarker supports video processing with configurable detection thresholds [@google2026facelandmarker]. For a presence pilot, a useful output is a sustained interval during which the system could not locate a face. Its interpretation depends on whether the camera provided usable frames. Occlusion or poor light can interrupt tracking, and looking away can be consistent with permitted work.

A virtual camera can legitimately supply a composed scene as a webcam input [@obs2022virtualcamera]. FaceForensics++ and the DeepFake Detection Challenge provide examples for studying image manipulation and face swaps [@rossler2019faceforensics] [@dolhansky2020dfdc]. A classifier evaluated on neighbouring frames from the same videos can appear to generalize even when it mainly recognizes those videos. Assessing it on new identities, source videos and manipulation methods would be necessary before using its output in examinations.

Proctoring research identifies privacy, fairness and trust as issues for students whose work is flagged [@coghlan2021proctor]. The project review identifies Kazakhstan's personal-data law as the starting point for institutional decisions about collection, storage and access [@kazakhstan2013personal]. It treats the EU AI Act as a comparator whose application depends on the deployment [@european2024aiact]. For a face-presence experiment, the practical choices are consented recordings, limited retention, teacher inspection of time intervals and an opportunity for students to explain the circumstances. [Ethical and legal discussion](../05_literature/anomaly_detection.md)

## Choices for Examory

The measured comparison supports quantile rules as the primary behavioural baseline under the studied training conditions. Examory retains the forest as a comparator so that the effect of peers and training contamination can be tested. For text, windowed MinHash gives inspectable candidate pairs, but missed the project's recall targets on the selected PAN documents. It remains a measured retrieval baseline. For code, the first comparison uses winnowing because it can point to both original fragments. Generated-writing indicators require offline false-positive measurements, and the initial video work concerns sustained face presence. These decisions specify what evidence a teacher can inspect and where the current data still leave a question open.
