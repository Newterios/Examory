# Model cards for Examory's integrity and behavioral detectors

A teacher can use these outputs to select work for review. Behavioral labels in the experiments describe simulated mechanisms. Similarity labels describe passages or functions. Decisions about attribution, authorized collaboration and assessment conditions require the teacher's judgment.

## Quantile rules for quiz attempts

The rules rank an attempt by the largest two-sided empirical tail departure across timing, revision, grade-history and peer features. Normal training cohorts define the feature distributions. Validation cohorts set operating thresholds; a tie at the cutoff receives the same decision for all students. The published inflation study used 20 SIM-BL worlds of 2,000 students and three sittings, with item and response parameters calibrated using EdNet. At its nominal 5% FPR threshold, mean recall was **36.2%** (95% across-seed interval **33.0–39.3%**), and realized FPR was **5.2%** (**4.5–5.8%**). [Main evaluation](experiments/inflation/results/tables/main.md)

On a separate seed-42 world, slow students had the highest FPR of the three prespecified strata, **10/165 (6.06%)**, compared with **28/645 (4.34%)** overall. Students with unusual but legitimate response times may be flagged. In the ten-attempt E-04 integration fixture, the proposed rules publisher alerted on both designated fast attempts and four of eight designated normal attempts. That calibration failed the fixture's two-alert expectation. The M4 scorer in PR #31 publishes forest signals. Being unusually fast or slow cannot establish intent. ## Isolation Forest for quiz attempts

The baseline fits 200 trees to normal training-cohort attempts and uses separate validation thresholds. The public ranking is relative to training scores. A high rank asks for a closer look at the attempt and its question times. On the same 20 SIM-BL seeds, mean recall was **27.8%** (95% across-seed interval **25.8–29.7%**). On the separate seed-42 world, FPR reached **9/72 (12.50%)** for fast, strong students, compared with **39/645 (6.05%)** overall. Those traits come from simulation truth for evaluation only. A small subgroup and repeated sittings of the same students limit generalization. [Main evaluation](experiments/inflation/results/tables/main.md) · [Stratum counts](https://github.com/Newterios/Examory/blob/9993e9ef63cfc92f21002e9be340825c059c2a1f/ml/experiments/behavior/results.md)

The original reader expects one point per answered question and complete responses. The v2 reader accepts weighted, partial and timed-out attempts with observable answer timing. Both readers gave identical forest scores on 60 complete attempts from one held-out cohort. On 24 simulated final preknowledge attempts, the forest found three and the experimental gain rule found 11. Changes in scores can also reflect practice. Neither result supports an inference of misconduct for an individual student. [Live-reader comparison](https://github.com/Newterios/Examory/blob/794e8cad175907b1429c9ef784a049cac8f28cac/ml/experiments/behavior/live_results.md) · [Gain-rule comparison](https://github.com/Newterios/Examory/blob/9993e9ef63cfc92f21002e9be340825c059c2a1f/ml/experiments/behavior/results.md)

## Windowed MinHash for text overlap

Five-token shingles, 200-token windows and 128 permutations produce candidate documents. The pipeline checks candidates with exact Jaccard similarity and maps overlapping spans back to the original text. It indexes a comparison corpus; validation on held-out document families selects similarity and minimum-span cutoffs. The PAN-PC-11 experiment excluded oversized connected families and evaluated reuse of at least 50 tokens. Across five 360-document samples, candidate recall ranged from **0 to 33.3%**. No pair survived final verification. On seed 42, candidate recall was **1/4 (25%)**, with a family-bootstrap 95% interval **0–100%**; pair recall was **0% [0%, 0%]** among draws with a positive denominator. Precision was undefined. These small family counts limit inference. [PAN evaluation and intervals](https://github.com/Newterios/Examory/blob/c4c4a020eb3ced0a50b63c2a1c59414107f44497/ml/integrity/results.md)

The test did not estimate student subgroup FPR. Template overlap, permitted quotations and conventional wording can all produce shared text. Paraphrasing and other forms of obfuscation reduce lexical overlap and can hide a source. A missing match therefore gives weak evidence about originality. [Obfuscation results](https://github.com/Newterios/Examory/blob/c4c4a020eb3ced0a50b63c2a1c59414107f44497/ml/integrity/results.md)

## Winnowing for Java code overlap

This detector normalizes Java identifiers and literals, fingerprints five-token grams using four-gram windows and returns aligned character spans. Validation selected a fingerprint Jaccard cutoff of **0.20**. In a BigCloneBench-derived sample, 763 functions formed 256 validation and 256 held-out labelled pairs. Test precision was **0.9794 [0.937, 1.000]**, recall **0.9896 [0.962, 1.000]**, F1 **0.9845 [0.958, 0.997]** and FPR **0.0625 [0.000, 0.353]**. Brackets give 95% bootstrap intervals over test sampling groups. [Java benchmark and intervals](https://github.com/Newterios/Examory/blob/97128dc46387290b7ec0d84c4a01a6064fa6454a/ml/integrity/code/results.md)

Four of 64 labelled nonclones matched. The shared negative set precludes a distinct FPR by clone type; student subgroup FPR was unmeasured. T3 results cover strong and moderate clones. The supplied-pair benchmark does not measure repository-wide retrieval, and shared boilerplate or a required algorithm can yield similar code. A match identifies code to inspect alongside the assignment and its allowed resources.
